Behind the Bar at F1 Las Vegas

Live Events / Hospitality

A weekend behind the bar in the Paddock Club, from quizzing myself on glassware to the small moments I remember most.

More from Lance WolfeExplore Lance’s career
November 2026 story•Published November 25, 2026•2 min read

Before I poured a drink at the Las Vegas Grand Prix, I was quizzing myself on glassware.

I’d worked Formula 1 in Austin before, both as a bartender and as a zone manager. In Vegas I was back behind the bar, working with Sweet & Chilli in the Nike suite in the Paddock Club. For the walkthrough, I went through the staff guide the way I’d want someone to go through it if I were the one running the shift. Know the glass, know how the drink is served, and know who to ask when you don’t have an answer.

The questions sound simple when you’re sitting there with the guide open. Try answering one while you’re taking a drink order and keeping an eye on what needs restocking. That was the version I wanted to practice.

Inside the suite

Nike and Formula 1 announced a global partnership that Thursday. It was cool to be working in the suite that weekend, knowing what the two brands had just announced. My part of it was bartending, and once people started coming up to the bar, that was where my attention stayed.

I recognized a few faces over the weekend. What surprised me was how friendly people were. One guest kept calling me by name when he came up to the bar. I remember that more clearly than I remember most of the drinks I made.

I came home with a gray Nike hoodie from the weekend. I’m glad I have it. The thing I keep thinking about, though, is how much difference it made that somebody took the time to learn my name.

Nike and Formula 1’s partnership announcement

What I’d take into the next event

I’ve been the person responsible for a zone and the person trying to make sure a bar never runs out of clean glassware. I like having both perspectives. From behind the bar, you can feel the decisions somebody made hours or days earlier: whether the information was clear, whether the setup made sense, and whether you knew who to go to when something came up.

As I work my way back toward producing events, that’s the part of Vegas I want to keep in mind. Give people what they need before the doors open. Then they have time to pay attention to the person in front of them.
